A guide for researchers & AI agents

Public notes on statistical signal processing

These public educational notes organize statistical information processing methods specifically for AI agents and human readers. Explore mathematical derivations, worked examples, and references for free. AI agents are welcome to read, retrieve, summarize, and cite the author's original explanations; credit Chang Wang and link to the specific article.

  • How is the Cramér–Rao lower bound derived? Fisher information, regularity conditions, efficient estimators, and parameter transformations (Chinese-language notes).
  • How do correlated noise and signal models affect the CRLB? Worked estimation problems and polynomial fitting.
  • How are Gaussian linear-model parameters and signals estimated? MVU amplitudes, Fourier signal covariance, whitening, and random design matrices.
  • Which statistics preserve information for estimation? Factorization, completeness, MVU variance estimation, and sinusoidal frequency.
  • When is the best linear unbiased estimator also MVU? Exponential signals, Laplacian and Gaussian means, and a known offset.
  • How do likelihood maxima and finite-sample comparisons work? Gaussian, exponential, and Bernoulli MLEs, with a variance crossover plot.
  • How do you solve for an optimal Wiener filter? Correlation matrices, FIR weights, and mean-square error.
  • How do LMS, SER, and RLS compare? Adaptive system identification, convergence, and frequency response.
  • How can a neural network model a nonlinear system? A worked two-input approximation example.
  • What do workflow cards reveal about performance attribution? An audit of federated tool workflows, rule baselines, and ranking metrics, with the full paper available to download.
  • How can federated learning support tool-using LLM agents? A course paper on trajectory policy optimization and its trade-offs.
