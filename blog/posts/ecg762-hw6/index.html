ECG 762 HW6 — BLUE for Exponential Signals, Laplacian and Gaussian Means, and a Known Offset

Chapter 6 studies the best linear unbiased estimator (BLUE). The three problems show how signal energy controls variance, why Gaussian assumptions matter when moving from BLUE to MVU, and how a known offset is removed in an affine estimator.

Problem 6.1 — An exponential signal in white noise

For known real $r$, observe $x[n]=Ar^n+w[n]$, where the noise is zero-mean white with variance $\sigma^2>0$. Let $\mathbf s=[1,r,\ldots,r^{N-1}]^T$ and $\mathbf C=\sigma^2\mathbf I$. The scalar BLUE formula gives

$$\widehat A_{\mathrm{BLUE}}=\frac{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf x}{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf s}=\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r^n x[n]}{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r^{2n}}.$$

Taking expectations replaces $x[n]$ by $Ar^n$, proving unbiasedness. The minimum variance among linear unbiased estimators is

$$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat A_{\mathrm{BLUE}})=\frac{\sigma^2}{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}r^{2n}}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sigma^2\frac{1-r^2}{1-r^{2N}},&r^2\ne1,\\[4pt]\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^2}{N},&r^2=1.\end{cases}$$
  • If $|r|<1$, the variance tends to $\sigma^2(1-r^2)>0$.
  • If $|r|=1$, the variance is $\sigma^2/N\to0$.
  • If $|r|>1$, the variance is $\sigma^2(r^2-1)/(r^{2N}-1)\to0$.
ResultThe variance tends to zero exactly when $|r|\ge1$. A decaying signal has finite total energy, leaving a positive variance floor even with indefinitely many observations.

Problem 6.4 — BLUE versus MVU for two location models

For IID observations with mean $\mu$ and individual variance $v$, $E[\mathbf x]=\mu\mathbf1$ and $\mathbf C=v\mathbf I$. Therefore,

$$\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{BLUE}}=\frac{\mathbf1^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf x}{\mathbf1^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf1}=\overline x,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\overline x)=\frac vN.$$

Laplacian observations

For $p(x;\mu)=\tfrac12 e^{-|x-\mu|}$, symmetry gives mean $\mu$, and

$$v=\int_0^\infty z^2e^{-z}\,dz=2,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{BLUE}})=\frac2N.$$

Being best among linear unbiased estimators does not establish optimality among all unbiased estimators. For $N=3$, the sample median $M$ is unbiased by symmetry and

$$\operatorname{Var}(M)=\frac{23}{36}<\frac23=\operatorname{Var}(\overline x).$$

Thus the sample mean is not MVU in this case. The comparison alone does not establish that the median is MVU.

Gaussian observations

For $x[n]\sim\mathcal N(\mu,1)$, $v=1$ and the Fisher information is $I(\mu)=N$. The unbiased sample mean attains the CRLB:

$$\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{MVU}}=\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{BLUE}}=\overline x,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\overline x)=\frac1N.$$

Problem 6.6 — Affine BLUE with a known offset

Suppose $E[\mathbf x]=\theta\mathbf s+\beta\mathbf1$, with known $\beta$, positive-definite covariance $\mathbf C$, and $\mathbf s\ne\mathbf0$. Consider $\widehat\theta=\mathbf a^T\mathbf x+b$. Unbiasedness for every $\theta$ requires

$$\mathbf a^T\mathbf s=1,\qquad b=-\beta\mathbf a^T\mathbf1.$$

Every admissible estimator can therefore be written as $\widehat\theta=\mathbf a^T(\mathbf x-\beta\mathbf1)$, with variance $\mathbf a^T\mathbf C\mathbf a$. Minimize this quadratic form subject to $\mathbf a^T\mathbf s=1$ using

$$L(\mathbf a,\lambda)=\mathbf a^T\mathbf C\mathbf a-2\lambda(\mathbf a^T\mathbf s-1).$$

The stationarity condition $2\mathbf C\mathbf a-2\lambda\mathbf s=\mathbf0$ gives $\mathbf a=\lambda\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf s$. Applying the constraint yields

$$\lambda=\frac1{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf s},\qquad \mathbf a_{\mathrm{opt}}=\frac{\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf s}{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf s}.$$

Positive definiteness makes the objective strictly convex, so this stationary point is the global minimum:

$$\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{BLUE}}=\frac{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}(\mathbf x-\beta\mathbf1)}{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf s},\qquad b_{\mathrm{opt}}=-\beta\frac{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf1}{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf s}.$$ $$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{BLUE}})=\mathbf a_{\mathrm{opt}}^T\mathbf C\mathbf a_{\mathrm{opt}}=\frac1{\mathbf s^T\mathbf C^{-1}\mathbf s}.$$
Subtracting a known offset changes the mean but not the covariance. The offset is removed before estimation and does not change the minimum variance.
