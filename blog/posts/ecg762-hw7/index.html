ECG 762 HW7 — Maximum Likelihood Estimation and the Limits of Asymptotic Variance Comparisons

Chapter 7 derives maximum likelihood estimators and verifies that they maximize the likelihood. A finite-sample variance comparison illustrates why an asymptotically better estimator can still be worse at the sample size actually available.

Problem 7.3 — Gaussian and exponential MLEs

Gaussian mean with unit variance

For IID $\mathcal N(\mu,1)$ observations, the log-likelihood is

$$\ell(\mu)=-\frac N2\log(2\pi)-\frac12\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(x[n]-\mu)^2.$$ $$\ell'(\mu)=\sum_n x[n]-N\mu,\qquad \ell''(\mu)=-N<0.$$

Solving the score equation gives $\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{ML}}=\overline x$. Strict concavity proves that this is the unique global maximizer. It estimates the population mean by the sample mean.

Exponential rate

For positive IID samples with density $\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$, $\lambda>0$,

$$L(\lambda)=\lambda^N e^{-\lambda\sum_n x[n]},\qquad \ell(\lambda)=N\log\lambda-\lambda\sum_nx[n].$$ $$\ell'(\lambda)=\frac N\lambda-\sum_nx[n],\qquad \ell''(\lambda)=-\frac N{\lambda^2}<0.$$ $$\widehat\lambda_{\mathrm{ML}}=\frac N{\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]}=\frac1{\overline x}.$$

The positive estimate is the unique global maximizer. Since $E[x]=1/\lambda$, larger observations correspond to a smaller estimated rate.

Problem 7.4 — Finite-sample and asymptotic rankings

Two unbiased estimators have variances $V_1=2/N$ and $V_2=1/N+100/N^2$. Their MSEs equal their variances. Subtracting gives

$$V_2-V_1=\frac{100-N}{N^2}.$$
Sample sizeVariance comparisonBetter estimator
$1\le N<100$$V_1<V_2$$\widehat\theta_1$
$N=100$$V_1=V_2=0.02$Equal performance
$N>100$$V_2<V_1$$\widehat\theta_2$

Although $V_2/V_1\to1/2$ as $N\to\infty$, the second estimator is worse for $N<100$. The omitted term is not small relative to $1/N$ until $N$ is large compared with 100:

$$\frac{100/N^2}{1/N}=\frac{100}{N}.$$
An asymptotic ranking is not a finite-sample ranking. Keep the higher-order term when it is still material at the sample size of interest.

Problem 7.7 — The exponential-family likelihood equation

For IID observations from $p(x;\theta)=\exp[A(\theta)B(x)+C(x)+D(\theta)]$,

$$\ell(\theta)=A(\theta)\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}B(x[n])+\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}C(x[n])+ND(\theta).$$

Assuming differentiability and parameter-independent support, an interior MLE must satisfy

$$A'(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ML}})\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}B(x[n])+ND'(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ML}})=0.$$

If $A'(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ML}})\ne0$, this becomes

$$\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}B(x[n])=-\frac{D'(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ML}})}{A'(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{ML}})}.$$

For the Gaussian model, choose $A(\mu)=\mu$, $B(x)=x$, $C(x)=-x^2/2$, and $D(\mu)=-\mu^2/2-\tfrac12\log(2\pi)$. The equation reduces to $\sum_nx[n]-N\widehat\mu=0$.

For the exponential model, choose $A(\lambda)=-\lambda$, $B(x)=x$, $C(x)=0$, and $D(\lambda)=\log\lambda$. The equation becomes $-\sum_nx[n]+N/\widehat\lambda=0$.

These recover the estimators in Problem 7.3. A score-equation solution still requires a maximum check, and boundary maxima must be considered separately.

Problem 7.8 — Bernoulli MLE including boundaries

Let $K=\sum_nx[n]$ count successes in $N$ IID Bernoulli trials. For $0\le p\le1$,

$$L(p)=p^K(1-p)^{N-K}.$$

When $0<K<N$, the log-likelihood has derivatives

$$\ell'(p)=\frac Kp-\frac{N-K}{1-p},\qquad \ell''(p)=-\frac K{p^2}-\frac{N-K}{(1-p)^2}<0.$$

Setting the score to zero gives $K-Np=0$, hence the unique interior maximum is $K/N$. The boundary cases agree:

  • If $K=0$, $L(p)=(1-p)^N$ is maximized at $p=0$.
  • If $K=N$, $L(p)=p^N$ is maximized at $p=1$.
Result for every possible sample$$\widehat p_{\mathrm{ML}}=\frac KN=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n].$$The MLE is the observed success proportion, including all-failure and all-success samples.
