ECG 762 HW5 — Sufficient Statistics and MVU Estimation for Rayleigh, Exponential, Gaussian, Sinusoidal, and Bernoulli Models

Chapter 5 uses factorization to identify sufficient statistics, then combines completeness and unbiasedness to obtain MVU estimators. The frequency-estimation problem also clarifies why the full data are always sufficient, even when a useful reduction does not exist.

Problem 5.2 — Rayleigh scale

For independent Rayleigh observations with $x[n]>0$ and parameter $\sigma^2>0$,

$$p(x[n];\sigma^2)=\frac{x[n]}{\sigma^2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x[n]^2}{2\sigma^2}\right).$$

The joint density factors into

$$p(\mathbf x;\sigma^2)=(\sigma^2)^{-N}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\sum_n x[n]^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\prod_n x[n].$$

Include the positive-support indicator in the parameter-free factor. By the Neyman–Fisher factorization theorem,

$$T(\mathbf x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]^2$$

is sufficient for $\sigma^2$: the parameter-dependent part of the density uses the data only through this sum of squares.

Problem 5.3 — Exponential rate

For IID observations with density $p(x;\lambda)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$ on $x>0$, $\lambda>0$,

$$p(\mathbf x;\lambda)=\lambda^N\exp\!\left(-\lambda\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]\right).$$

The support is independent of $\lambda$, so the factorization theorem gives

$$T(\mathbf x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n].$$

For fixed $N$, the sample mean is an invertible rescaling of $T$ and is also sufficient.

Problem 5.6 — Gaussian variance with a known mean

Observe $x[n]=A+w[n]$, where $A$ is known and $w[n]$ are independent $\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ samples. The joint density is

$$p(\mathbf x;\sigma^2)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-N/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{\sum_n(x[n]-A)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right).$$

Thus $T=\sum_n(x[n]-A)^2$ is sufficient. Since $E[T]=N\sigma^2$, the estimator $T/N$ is unbiased. Under the problem's completeness assumption, the Rao–Blackwell–Lehmann–Scheffé theorem makes it MVU:

$$\widehat{\sigma^2}_{\mathrm{MVU}}=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(x[n]-A)^2.$$ $$\frac{T}{\sigma^2}\sim\chi_N^2,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat{\sigma^2}_{\mathrm{MVU}})=\frac{2\sigma^4}{N}.$$
Why the denominator is NThe mean is known. No degree of freedom is spent estimating it, so the denominator is $N$, rather than $N-1$.

Problem 5.7 — Sinusoidal frequency and minimal sufficiency

For $x[n]=\cos(2\pi f_0n)+w[n]$ with known Gaussian noise variance, expanding the likelihood leaves the parameter-dependent data term

$$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]\cos(2\pi f_0n).$$

This expression itself is not a statistic for the unknown frequency: its weights depend on $f_0$. More precisely, compare two observation vectors:

$$\log\frac{p(\mathbf x;f_0)}{p(\mathbf y;f_0)}=C(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)+\frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}(x[n]-y[n])\cos(2\pi f_0n).$$

When $f_0$ ranges over a nondegenerate interval, the functions $1,\cos(2\pi f_0),\ldots,\cos(2\pi(N-1)f_0)$ are linearly independent. The ratio is parameter-independent exactly when $x[n]=y[n]$ for every $n=1,\ldots,N-1$. Hence a minimal sufficient statistic is

$$T(\mathbf x)=(x[1],x[2],\ldots,x[N-1]).$$
The full observation vector is always sufficient. Here $x[0]=1+w[0]$ can be discarded because it is independent of the other observations and its distribution does not depend on frequency. All remaining observations must be preserved up to an invertible transformation. The conclusion concerns the absence of a nontrivial information-losing reduction, not the absence of every sufficient statistic.

Problem 5.9 — Bernoulli success probability

Let $x[n]\in\{0,1\}$ be IID Bernoulli observations with success probability $\theta$. Their joint probability mass function is

$$p(\mathbf x;\theta)=\theta^{T(\mathbf x)}(1-\theta)^{N-T(\mathbf x)},\qquad T(\mathbf x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n].$$

Factorization makes the number of successes sufficient. Since $E[T]=N\theta$, $T/N$ is unbiased; with the assumed completeness,

$$\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{MVU}}=\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n],\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{MVU}})=\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}{N}.$$

The MVU estimate is the observed proportion of successes.
